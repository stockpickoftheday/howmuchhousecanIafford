Buying a home in Ontario

Ontario charges land transfer tax on every home purchase, on a sliding scale. First-time buyers get up to $4,000 back — and buyers inside the City of Toronto pay a second, municipal tax on top.

Ontario land transfer tax rates

Each rate applies only to the part of the price inside that bracket, not to the whole price.

On a $500,000 home the tax is $275 + $1,950 + $2,250 + $2,000 = $6,475. On a $750,000 home it is $11,475.

First-time buyer rebate — up to $4,000

If you have never owned a home anywhere in the world, are 18 or older, a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, and will live in the home as your principal residence within nine months, Ontario refunds up to $4,000 of the tax. That covers the whole tax on a home up to about $368,000. Above that, the refund is still $4,000 and you pay the difference — so a first-time buyer pays $2,475 on a $500,000 home and $7,475 on a $750,000 home.

Buying in Toronto? There is a second tax

Inside the City of Toronto, the municipal land transfer tax applies on top of the provincial one, using the same brackets up to $3 million (with higher rates above $3 million since April 1, 2026). Toronto also has its own first-time buyer rebate of up to $4,475, so a qualifying Toronto buyer can save up to $8,475 in total. Everywhere else in Ontario, only the provincial tax applies.

Ontario's other closing cost: PST on the CMHC premium

Ontario is one of three provinces that charges sales tax on the CMHC insurance premium — 8% of the premium, payable in cash at closing. It cannot be added to the mortgage. On an $11,557 premium that is $925. The main calculator adds this line automatically when Ontario is selected.
