Buying a home in Yukon

Yukon has no land transfer tax. The Land Titles Office charges a flat fee by value band to register the transfer — $150 on most homes — plus a small assurance fund fee.

Land Titles transfer fees

The fee depends on the declared value of the land (what it would sell for on the open market), under the Land Titles Tariff of Fees Regulation.

Declared valueTransfer fee
Under $100,000$50
$100,000 to $499,999.99$150
$500,000 to $2,999,999.99$350
$3,000,000 to $9,999,999.99$550
$10,000,000 or more$750

Because the fee is a flat band rather than a percentage, it stays small even on expensive homes: a $500,000 home and a $2,000,000 home both pay $350 to transfer the title.

Two more fees to budget for

The transfer fee is not the whole bill. An assurance fund fee also applies: $20 plus $10 for each $10,000 (or part of $10,000) of the increase in value since the last transfer — so it depends on the property's history, and the calculator does not include it. A separate fee also applies to register the mortgage (roughly $100 to $200 on a typical mortgage). Both are small, but they are on top of the $150 above.

No first-time buyer relief

There is no first-time buyer relief on these fees, because there is no tax to rebate. Yukon Housing Corporation runs first-time homebuyer and down payment assistance programs instead.

More things to know

  • The assurance fund fee is charged on the increase in value since the last transfer, not on the full price — so a home that has not changed hands in years costs more to register than one that sold recently.
  • Some northern properties are on leased or Commissioner's land rather than fee simple title. Your lawyer will flag it — it changes what you are actually buying.
  • Most Yukon buyers buy in Whitehorse. The same federal mortgage rules apply there as everywhere in Canada.
