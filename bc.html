Buying a home in British Columbia

BC calls its land transfer tax the property transfer tax (PTT). It is usually the biggest closing cost after the down payment — but first-time buyers can pay nothing at all on a home up to $500,000.

BC property transfer tax rates

Each rate applies only to the part of the price inside that bracket, not to the whole price.

Part of the priceRate
First $200,0001%
$200,001 to $2,000,0002%
$2,000,001 to $3,000,0003%
Over $3,000,000 (residential)5%

On a $750,000 home the tax is 1% of $200,000 ($2,000) plus 2% of $550,000 ($11,000) — $13,000 in total. On a $1,500,000 home it is $2,000 + $26,000 = $28,000.

First-time buyer exemption

If you have never owned a home anywhere in the world, and the home will be your principal residence, the exemption is:

Fair market valueExemption
$500,000 or lessFull exemption — no tax to pay
Over $500,000 to $835,000$8,000 off (the tax on the first $500,000)
Over $835,000 to under $860,000Phases out proportionally
$860,000 or moreNo exemption

So a first-time buyer pays $5,000 on a $750,000 home ($13,000 tax minus the $8,000 exemption) and $0 on a $480,000 home. These are the rules in force since April 1, 2024 — the old $525,000 partial-exemption limit no longer applies. Buyers of newly built homes get a separate exemption: full relief up to $1,100,000, partial to $1,150,000.

Two more BC things to know

  • Property tax is low for the price. BC's residential property tax rates are among the lowest in Canada — roughly 0.3% to 0.5% of the value in Vancouver — which is why the calculator's $3,600-a-year default is about right for a mid-priced BC home.
  • Foreign buyers pay 20% extra. If you are not a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, an additional 20% tax applies on residential property in Metro Vancouver, the Fraser Valley, Victoria and some other areas.
