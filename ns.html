Buying a home in Nova Scotia

Nova Scotia has no provincial land transfer tax. Instead, each municipality charges its own deed transfer tax — and Halifax, where most buyers buy, charges the maximum: 1.5% of the price.

Deed transfer tax by municipality

The province caps the municipal rate at 1.5%. What you pay depends on where the home is. The figures below use Halifax's rate; confirm your municipality's rate before you budget.

MunicipalityRate
Halifax Regional Municipality1.5%
Cape Breton Regional Municipality1.5%
Some municipalities1.25%
Yarmouth, Pictou County and others1%

On a $500,000 home in Halifax the tax is $7,500. The same home in a 1% municipality is $5,000. On a $750,000 Halifax home it is $11,250.

No first-time buyer relief

Nova Scotia does not rebate deed transfer tax for first-time buyers. The province helps first-time buyers with the down payment instead, through the Down Payment Assistance Program — an interest-free 5% loan — plus the federal FHSA and Home Buyers' Plan. Note that buyers who are not Nova Scotia residents pay an extra 10% provincial deed transfer tax (since April 1, 2025), waived if you become a resident.

More things to know

  • The tax is due within 10 days of the transfer. Your lawyer collects it at closing.
  • Municipalities can change their rates without telling Service Nova Scotia, so confirm the rate for the exact municipality you are buying in.
  • Halifax property tax for 2026/27 is about $1.11 per $100 of assessed value in urban areas — higher than the calculator's $3,600-a-year default on a mid-priced home.

Figures verified November 2026. Tax rules change — confirm them with your lawyer or notary before closing. This page is educational only, not financial advice.
