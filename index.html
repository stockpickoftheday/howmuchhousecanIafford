How the number is worked out

The stress test

Your lender does not qualify you at the rate they offer. They qualify you at the higher of your rate plus 2%, or 5.25%. So a 5% mortgage means you must prove you could pay at 7%. That is the rule for every insured mortgage in Canada, and most lenders use it for uninsured ones too. It is the reason the maximum is lower than many people expect — it protects you if rates rise.

GDS and TDS — the 39% and 44% limits

Two ratios decide how much housing cost you can carry:

  • GDS (gross debt service), 39%: your housing costs — mortgage payment at the stress-test rate, property tax, heating, and half of any condo fees — must be no more than 39% of your gross monthly income.
  • TDS (total debt service), 44%: the same housing costs plus all your other monthly debt payments must be no more than 44% of your gross monthly income.

The calculator works out both and uses whichever gives the smaller number. In the example above, TDS is what limits the price, because of the $500 a month in other debts.

CMHC insurance

If your down payment is under 20% of the price, your mortgage must have default insurance — usually called CMHC insurance, though Sagen and Canada Guaranty sell the same thing. It protects the lender, not you. The premium is a percentage of the mortgage: 4.00% with 5% to 9.99% down, 3.10% with 10% to 14.99% down, and 2.80% with 15% to 19.99% down. Amortizations over 25 years add 0.20%. The premium is normally added to your mortgage, so you pay interest on it too. With 20% or more down, there is no premium at all. Insured mortgages are only available on homes under $1.5 million.

Down payment

The minimum down payment in Canada is 5% of the first $500,000 of the price, plus 10% of the part above $500,000. So a $750,000 home needs at least $50,000 down. A bigger down payment helps twice: it lowers your mortgage, and it can drop you into a cheaper CMHC premium tier — crossing from 9.99% to 10% down cuts the premium rate from 4.00% to 3.10%.

Land transfer tax

It depends on where you buy. BC, Ontario, Manitoba, Quebec, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia and Prince Edward Island charge a tax on the price; Alberta, Saskatchewan, Newfoundland and Labrador and the three territories charge registration fees instead. First-time buyers pay less in BC, Ontario and PEI, and get a tax credit back later in Quebec. Pick your province in the calculator to see your number, or read the province pages: British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Ontario, Quebec, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, Newfoundland and Labrador, Yukon, Northwest Territories, Nunavut.

Cash to close

The down payment is not the whole bill. On closing day you also need the land transfer tax (after any first-time buyer relief), about $1,500 in legal fees, and the CMHC premium if you have one. The calculator adds them up, and also shows the smaller out-of-pocket number for the usual case where the premium is rolled into the mortgage.

This calculator is an estimate for education only. It is not financial advice, and a lender's real answer depends on your credit, your job history and the home itself. Confirm the numbers with a mortgage broker or your bank before you make an offer.
