Buying a home in Prince Edward Island

PEI charges a flat 1% real property transfer tax — but qualifying first-time buyers pay nothing at all, whatever the price. It is the most generous first-time buyer exemption in Canada.

The 1% tax

The tax is 1% of the greater of the price you paid or the assessed value. No tax at all is payable if that amount is $30,000 or less.

The first-time buyer exemption

To qualify you must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, have lived in PEI for at least six months before the deed is registered (or have filed a PEI tax return in at least two of the previous six years), never have held an interest in a principal residence, and live in the home as your principal residence for at least 183 consecutive days. Every buyer named on the deed must qualify. If you have not reached six months of residency on closing day, you can claim a refund once you have lived in the home for 183 days. There is no price cap — the regulation that once capped the exemption at $200,000 was revoked on October 1, 2016.

More things to know

  • There is no price cap on the exemption. A first-time buyer pays $0 on a $300,000 home and $0 on a $1,500,000 home.
  • Newcomers beware: a purchase inside your first six months on the Island does not qualify, however new to home ownership you are.
  • No sales tax on the CMHC premium in PEI. Only Ontario, Quebec and Saskatchewan tax it.
