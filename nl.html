Buying a home in Newfoundland and Labrador

Newfoundland and Labrador has no land transfer tax. What you pay instead are Registry of Deeds fees — about 0.4% of the value, charged twice: once on the deed, once on the mortgage. It is one of the cheapest provinces in Canada to close a purchase.

Registry of Deeds fees

The same formula applies to both registrations: $100 if the value is $500 or less, otherwise $100 plus $0.40 for each additional $100 (or part of $100) of value. The mortgage fee is capped at $5,000.

On a $350,000 home the deed alone costs $1,498. With a minimum down payment, the two fees together come to about $2,979.

No first-time buyer relief

There is no first-time buyer relief on these fees, because there is no transfer tax to exempt — the fee is the same modest amount for every buyer. There is also no sales tax on the CMHC premium in Newfoundland and Labrador.

More things to know

  • The fees grow with the price and the loan. A bigger mortgage means a bigger mortgage fee — the fee is charged on the amount the mortgage secures, including any CMHC premium added to it.
  • The mortgage fee is capped at $5,000. The deed fee has no cap, but at 0.4% it stays modest.
  • St. John's benchmark price was about $425,400 in August 2026 — close to the $422,815 maximum in the example below.
