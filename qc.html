Buying a home in Quebec

Quebec calls its land transfer tax the welcome tax — droit de mutation immobilière. It is charged by the municipality, not the province, and the bill arrives in the mail after closing, not on closing day.

Quebec welcome tax rates (2026)

Each rate applies only to the part of the price inside that bracket. These are the provincial base brackets — they apply in every Quebec municipality.

On a $500,000 home the tax is $5,611 anywhere in Quebec. On a $600,000 home it is $7,111 — unless you are in Montreal.

Montreal charges more

Montreal adds higher tiers on expensive homes. Other cities, including Laval, Quebec City and Longueuil, add their own tiers above $500,000 too, but Montreal goes highest.

On a $600,000 home in Montreal the tax is $7,349 (vs $7,111 elsewhere). On a $750,000 home it is $10,349 in Montreal (vs $9,361 elsewhere). On a $1,000,000 home it is $15,349 in Montreal (vs $13,111 elsewhere).

First-time buyers: a credit back later, not a discount now

Since January 1, 2026, Quebec refunds first-time buyers part of the welcome tax through a refundable tax credit of up to $5,875: 100% of the first $5,000 of duties plus 25% of the next $3,500. It phases out above a $750,000 price and is nil at $1,000,000. You must intend to live in the home as your principal residence, and neither you nor your spouse can have lived in a home you owned in the year of purchase or the four years before. You pay the municipal bill in full first and claim the credit on your Quebec income tax return — so it does not reduce the cash you need at closing. From October 2026, Revenu Québec will advance the credit once the bill is paid, but only if it is more than $1,000. Montreal's own municipal first-time buyer program has ended.

Quebec taxes the CMHC premium too

The CMHC premium itself is added to your mortgage, but Quebec's tax on insurance premiums is not — you pay it in cash at the notary. The rate is 9% for premiums paid by December 31, 2026, rising to 9.975% for premiums paid after that date. On the $11,557 premium in the example below, that is $1,040 in 2026.

More things to know

  • The bill comes after closing. The municipality mails it once the deed is registered, and payment is due within about 30 days — budget for it even though it is not on the closing-day bill.
  • The tax is charged on the highest of three numbers: the price you paid, the amount in the deed, or the municipal assessment times the city's comparative factor (1.00 in Montreal for 2026).
  • New homes also pay GST (5%) and QST (9.975%) on the price, with partial rebates — a separate cost your notary will work out.
