Buying a home in New Brunswick

New Brunswick charges a flat 1% real property transfer tax — no brackets, no first-time buyer break. It is the simplest transfer tax in Canada: 1% of the price, full stop.

The 1% tax

The tax is 1% of the greater of the price you paid or the assessed value of the property, and it is payable before the deed is registered — so it comes out of your closing funds.

Registration is separate: $84 per parcel per document, so a one-parcel purchase with a mortgage pays $84 to register the transfer and $84 to register the mortgage — $168 in total.

No first-time buyer relief

New Brunswick offers no exemption, rebate or refund on the transfer tax. First-time buyers pay the same 1% as everyone else. The help for first-time buyers comes from federal programs: the First Home Savings Account (FHSA), the Home Buyers' Plan (RRSP), and the GST rebate on new homes.

More things to know

  • The rate doubled in 2016. It was 0.5% before April 1, 2016 — older pages still quote 0.5%, and they are a decade out of date.
  • The assessed value matters. If the province assesses the home above what you paid, the tax is charged on the assessment.
  • The 2026 assessment freeze does not apply to homes that just sold. If you buy in 2026, your property tax is based on a new, current assessment.

Figures verified November 2026. Tax rules change — confirm them with your lawyer or notary before closing. This page is educational only, not financial advice.
